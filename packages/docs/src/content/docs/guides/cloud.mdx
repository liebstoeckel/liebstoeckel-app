A built deck is one self-contained `.html` you can host anywhere. **liebstoeckel cloud**
(the hosted control plane) adds a private, always-on home for your decks behind a login.
For teams, it adds a shared library with members, roles, and invitations.

<Aside type="caution" title="Coming soon">
liebstoeckel cloud is not generally available yet. The CLI already ships the cloud
commands (`login`, `push`, `orgs`, `decks`, `brand`), but there is no public hosted
service to sign in to. Everything on this page describes the hosted layer as it will
launch; the rest of the framework works fully offline today.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note">
The framework is MPL-2.0. The cloud is the operated layer; the CLI talks to it over a
versioned REST API, so it can also point at a private deployment of the control plane.
</Aside>

## Sign in from the terminal

Authentication is passwordless. `login` runs the OAuth 2.0 device-authorization grant:
the CLI prints a URL, you open it, sign in with a one-time email code, and approve.

```bash
liebstoeckel login --api https://<your-control-plane-host>
```

The token is stored in `~/.config/liebstoeckel/credentials.json` (mode `600`).

A new account accepts the terms of service once, in the dashboard, before it can create
or change anything. Until then, reading works (`decks`, `orgs`, `pull`), but `push`,
moves, deletes and every other change are refused with the code `terms_not_accepted`
and a link to the dashboard: open it, sign in with the same email, accept, and run the
command again. `dev --live` follows the live deck read-only until then.

## Push a deck

```bash
liebstoeckel build          # → ./dist/<deck-slug>.html
liebstoeckel push           # uploads the built deck in ./dist
```

The deck's **title** comes from its embedded `<title>` (or an explicit `--title`
override), and its **cover thumbnail** is read from the slide thumbnails already baked
into the file, so there is nothing extra to send. The server reads both straight out of
the uploaded file, which means titles with em-dashes, smart quotes, or emoji just work.
The deck appears in your dashboard immediately.

A deck built without thumbnails (on a machine without Chromium, for example) still gets
them: the cloud captures the slides after the upload, and the cover shows up in the
dashboard a few seconds later, marked *Preparing cover* until then. The same happens for
decks written with the assistant or edited in the dashboard. The captured thumbnails are
embedded into the stored deck, so its presenter overview has them too. A new version
keeps showing the previous cover until its own is ready. Captures are free, but they add
their images to the deck's stored size.

## Organizations: personal and teams

Every account has a **personal workspace** (just you) and can create or join **team
organizations**. A deck always belongs to exactly one organization, the *active* one.

- In the **dashboard**, switch the active workspace with the switcher in the top-left.
- From the **CLI**, list your workspaces and choose where `push` lands:

```bash
liebstoeckel orgs            # list workspaces; → marks the push default
liebstoeckel orgs use acme   # make the "acme" team the default
liebstoeckel push --org acme # …or target a team for one push
```

## Teams: shared library, members & roles

Every deck pushed to a team is visible to **all its members** as a shared library, with
"uploaded by" attribution. Who can do what is governed by three roles:

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| **owner** | everything, incl. manage any deck, manage members, delete the org |
| **admin** | manage any deck, invite/remove members, change roles |
| **member** | upload decks; manage **their own** decks; view all team decks |

Manage all of this on the dashboard's **Team** page:

- **Invite a colleague** by email. They get a link, sign in with a one-time code, and join
  automatically (still passwordless).
- **Change roles** or **remove** members.
- **Leave** a team you no longer need (your personal workspace always stays).

### Deleting a workspace

An **owner** can delete a workspace, a team or their personal one, at the foot of the
**Team** page. Admins and members can't. You confirm by typing the workspace's name.

- **While a paid plan runs, deletion is refused.** Cancel the plan on the **Billing** page
  first; once the plan has ended, you can delete the workspace.
- **It is gone for everyone at once.** Members lose access, its share links, previews and
  live links stop working, running live sessions end, and pushes or API calls to it are
  refused. Every member gets an email saying who deleted it and until when it can be
  restored.
- **Owners can restore it for 14 days**, from the workspace switcher (deleted workspaces
  are listed there with their deadline) or from the link in their email. Everything comes
  back as it was, except live sessions that were running, which stay ended.
- **After 14 days everything in it is removed for good**: its decks with every version,
  share and preview links, live sessions and their saved results, brands, members,
  invitations and billing records. Until then the data is still held, so it can be
  restored.
- **Your account stays.** If it was your only workspace, you stay signed in and can start
  a new one, or restore the old one while that's still possible.

## Your account

Open **Account** from the menu behind your avatar (top right; on a phone, the ⋮ menu).

- **Passkeys:** add a passkey to sign in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN
  instead of an emailed code, and remove the ones you no longer use. Sign-in codes keep
  working either way. Adding a passkey needs a recent sign-in, and we email you when one
  is added. On the sign-in page, choose **Sign in with a passkey**.
- **Where you're signed in:** every browser and CLI session of your account. Sign out
  one of them, or choose **Sign out everywhere**, which also signs out this browser and
  the CLI and disconnects connected assistants (they ask you to connect again).
- **Recent activity:** sign-ins (with browser and platform), sign-ins from a new device,
  warnings about wrong codes, signing out everywhere and new passkeys, kept for 90 days.

### Sign-in emails and protection

We email you, from the same address as your sign-in codes, when:

- your account is signed in to from a browser or device it hasn't used in the last 90
  days (the first `liebstoeckel login` on a machine counts as one). The mail names the
  browser and platform and the time in UTC. If it wasn't you, open **Account** and choose
  **Sign out everywhere**.
- several wrong sign-in codes are entered for your address within an hour. Nobody can
  sign in without a code sent to you or one of your passkeys, so this needs no action if
  it was you.

These emails never ask you to click through to sign in or to enter a code.

The sign-in page runs a short, invisible check in your browser before it sends a code
(no cookies, nothing sent to anyone else). It needs a current browser; if it fails, reload
the page. To keep inboxes from being flooded, an address gets at most five codes in 15
minutes: use the newest one.

A new workspace's free AI credits start once you have accepted the terms of service.

### Download your data

**Download my data** prepares a zip with your account details (email, name, passkeys by
name, sessions), your recent security activity, every version of the terms you accepted, the workspaces you belong to
with your role, and the decks of every workspace you **own**: the current version of each
deck, the spec it was generated from, and its sources when you edit it in the browser.
Nothing about other people is in it. Decks in teams you are only a member of are not
included; ask an owner there.

The zip is prepared in the background and we email you when it's ready. The download
works for 7 days, and only while you are signed in to the account that asked for it.

### Deleting your account

At the foot of the **Account** page. You confirm by typing your email, from a sign-in no
older than a day (otherwise sign out and in again first).

- **Refused while** you are the only owner of a team that has other members (make someone
  else an owner, or delete the team), you are the only member of a team (delete that team
  first), or a paid plan runs on your personal workspace (cancel it on the **Billing**
  page first). The page lists what stops it, with a link to fix each.
- **At once:** your personal workspace is deleted the way any workspace is (its decks,
  share links and previews stop working, running live sessions end), you are signed out
  everywhere, connected assistants lose access, and you can no longer sign in. You get an
  email with a link to restore your account.
- **Teams:** you leave every team when your account is removed. The decks you made there
  stay with the team and show as made by "a former member". In the editing history of
  decks whose sources you edited (in any team, also ones you left earlier), your name and
  email are replaced by a code, like the records below.
- **Restore for 14 days** from the link in the email; it brings back your account and your
  personal workspace. Lost the email? Sign in with your address as usual: we tell you the
  account was deleted and send the link again.
- **After 14 days your account is removed for good**, with your personal workspace. We keep
  a record of which terms you accepted and your usage and payment records in teams, but not
  under your name or email address: they carry a code that only your email address can be
  matched against.

## Folders

With more decks, group them in **folders**. Folders nest like a file system, and a deck
lives in exactly one place: a folder, or the **root**, where every deck starts unless you
choose otherwise. Folders are purely for order: everyone in the workspace still sees every
deck, and who may manage a deck does not change.

```bash
liebstoeckel push --folder "Sales/Q4"         # a new deck goes there; missing folders are created
liebstoeckel decks                            # each deck with its key and folder
liebstoeckel decks move pitch --to "Sales/Q4" # move one or more decks (by key or id)
liebstoeckel folders                          # the folder tree
liebstoeckel folders delete "Sales/Q4"        # its decks move up to "Sales"; none is deleted
```

- **Pushing again never moves a deck.** `--folder` only places a *new* deck; for a deck that
  already exists it is ignored with a note, so a teammate's push can't undo your filing.
  Moving is always explicit.
- **Who can do what:** any member creates and renames folders, deletes an empty one, and
  moves the decks they may manage (their own; owners and admins: any). Deleting a folder
  that still holds decks or subfolders is for owners and admins, who choose where the
  content goes (by default the parent folder).
- **Bulk moves and deletes are all or nothing:** if one deck is not yours to manage,
  nothing changes and you're told which deck stopped it. `decks delete` asks before
  deleting; pass `--yes` in scripts.

In the **dashboard**, the folder tree sits on the left and every folder has its own address,
so you can bookmark or share a link to it. From there:

- **Create, rename and delete** folders from the tree, with the menu next to each folder. In the
  dashboard a folder's content moves up to its parent when it is deleted.
- **Move decks** by dragging them onto a folder in the tree, or with **Move to…** in a deck's
  menu, which also works with a keyboard or on a phone.
- **Select several decks** with their checkboxes (or **Select all**) to move or delete them in
  one go. Only decks you may manage can be selected.
- **Switch between grid and list** with the buttons above the decks; the choice is remembered
  in this browser.
- **Search** at the top finds deck titles and folder names across the whole workspace and shows
  where each one lives.
- Inside a folder, the assistant button reads **New deck in** and the folder's name, and the
  new deck starts in that folder.

## Share a deck publicly

Any deck can be published to a time-limited public link served on a separate domain, with
provenance chrome and an abuse-report path. Open a deck's **Share** action in the dashboard,
turn **Share with a link** on, and copy the link. The settings below it save as you change
them, and the link itself stays the same:

- **Expiry**: how long the link lives (7 days on the free plan; longer, or *no expiry*, on
  paid).

The "Published with liebstoeckel" banner is not a share setting. It follows the workspace's
plan: on the free plan every public link carries it, and on paid plans (**Pro** / **Team**)
no public link does, with nothing to turn on. If a workspace moves back to free, the banner
returns on all of its links. The abuse-report link is on every public share, whatever the
plan. Live sessions follow the same rule.

## Present live from the dashboard

Turn any hosted deck into a **live, interactive session** straight from the browser, with
no CLI and no local runtime. Use a deck's **Present** action, or **Start live session** on
the deck's own page:

- A **presenter view** opens (you drive the slides), and a **shareable audience link**
  appears that you can copy or hand out. Your audience opens it on any device and follows
  along, voting in polls, asking questions, and reacting in real time.
- Press **End session** when you're done. The audience link stops working immediately.

The deck runs in an isolated sandbox for the audience, and the relay that carries the
session **never executes deck code**, the same isolation as a [public relay](/guides/relay/).
Audience members can only interact through plugins (votes, questions, reactions); they can't
drive navigation or tamper with results.

**Free** gives you a real, usable live session with a capped audience and duration, and
**results aren't kept** once the session ends. **Pro / Team** raise the limits, drop the
audience watermark, and **persist results**: open a past session under **Present live** on
the deck's page to see decoded poll tallies, the Q&A backlog, and reaction totals.

### What live sessions store

A live session keeps what the audience sends through its plugins: **poll votes**,
**reactions**, and **Q&A questions** with their upvotes. The audience never signs in and
there is no name field: each browser gets a random participant id, and a question is
labelled with a generated handle such as `viewer-3f2a`. The only personal data a session
can hold is whatever a participant writes into a question.

- **Free**: results aren't kept. They are deleted shortly after the session's time limit
  runs out.
- **Pro / Team**: results are saved for **365 days** after the session ends. Each past
  session on the deck's page shows the date it is saved until.
- **After a withdrawal**: when a paid plan ends through a withdrawal (see
  [Withdrawing within 14 days](#withdrawing-within-14-days)), saved results are **kept for 30
  more days** and stay readable, even though the workspace is on Free by then. Subscribe again
  within those 30 days and nothing is lost; after them the free plan's rule applies. The
  Billing page shows the date. An ordinary cancellation at the end of the paid month has no
  such extra time.
- **The audience is told.** In a Pro / Team session, the Q&A box shows a short line above
  the input: "Your question may be saved by the presenter's organisation for up to a
  year." Free sessions don't show it, since nothing is kept. The line comes from the Q&A
  plugin inside the deck, so a deck you uploaded already built shows it only after you
  rebuild it with the current CLI and upload it again.
- **Delete results**: open a past session under **Present live** and choose **Delete
  results**. Poll votes, questions and reaction totals from that session are
  deleted for good; the session itself stays in the list. Anyone who can manage the deck
  can do this. If a participant asks for their question to be removed, this is how
  the presenter does it.
- **Deleting a deck** deletes its live sessions and their saved results too. When an
  organization is deleted, its live results are deleted with it once its 14-day restore
  window has passed (see [Deleting a workspace](#deleting-a-workspace)).
- **Moving to the free plan** applies the free rule to past sessions as well: their saved
  results are deleted shortly after the change. Copy anything you want to keep before you
  downgrade.

<Aside type="note">
Built-in plugins (poll, Q&A, reactions) sync purely peer-to-peer over the shared document,
so hosted live presenting needs no server-side plugin code. Plugins with custom server logic
run in the presenter's own browser tab.
</Aside>

## Shared brand

Your org is an **authenticated registry**, speaking the same protocol as `liebstoeckel add`
but private to your team. A **brand** (a theme token set: colours, fonts, glow, and an
ordered **chart palette**) is its first item type; custom company components can ride the
same rails later.

- **Define it** once: push a `defineTheme(...)` file (`liebstoeckel brand push ./brand.ts
  --default`) or edit it visually in the dashboard's **Brand** page.
- **Use it**: `liebstoeckel brand pull <name>` writes `brands/<name>.ts` into a deck, and
  `liebstoeckel new` auto-applies the org **default** brand. The brand is **owned source,
  baked into the deck at build**: self-contained and WYSIWYG, never a serve-time surprise.
- **Update it**: change the brand centrally, then authors (or CI) **re-pull + rebuild**.
  Deliberate, not silent.

Shared brand is a Pro/Team feature.

### Generate a starting point

Not sure where to start? The Brand page's **Generate** button rolls a complete brand
(colours, fonts, glow, and chart palette) in one click. It is *constrained*, not random:
a single seed picks a base hue, a colour-harmony scheme (analogous / complementary /
triadic / …), and a font-pairing recipe (minimal / editorial / modern / …), then every
token is derived by rule in the perceptual **OKLCH** space, with each colour floored to a
legible contrast on the dark background. Fonts are drawn from the catalog, so a generated
brand always ships its webfonts on `pull`. Re-roll until you like it, then tweak any field
by hand. The generated values are an editable starting point, never locked in.

### Fonts

The Brand page's **Heading**, **Body**, and **Mono** fields are a picker over a curated
catalog of [Fontsource](https://fontsource.org) variable fonts (grouped sans / serif /
display / monospace), with a **Custom…** option for any other `font-family` you type. The
preview shows the real typeface as you choose.

When you `liebstoeckel brand pull <name>`, a catalog font comes with its webfont: the
generated `brands/<name>.ts` `import`s the matching `@fontsource-variable/*` package, and
`pull` runs `bun add` to install it, so the font is **inlined into your single-file deck at
build** with no CDN involved. `liebstoeckel new` does the same for the org default brand.
Pass `--no-install` to write the files and install the font packages yourself. A
**Custom…** font carries no package; supply its `@font-face` yourself, or it falls back to
the system font.

If you author a brand as a file instead (`brand push ./brand.ts`), any font family that
isn't in the catalog is accepted but **flagged with a warning** (with a *did you mean* when
it's close, e.g. `"Inter"` → `"Inter Variable"`), so the file path is as honest as the
picker about which fonts actually ship a webfont.

### Chart palette

A brand also carries an ordered **chart-series palette** (`viz`): the colours charts cycle
through, exposed as `--brand-viz-0..n`. Edit it on the Brand page (add / reorder / remove
swatches) or set `viz: [...]` in a pushed `defineTheme(...)`. It's baked into pulled decks
so charts match the brand, not just the page chrome.

## Versions

A deck has a **stable identity** across re-pushes. `push` keys a deck by its folder name
(or `--name`), so editing and pushing again **updates the same deck at the same URL**;
the share link and view counts carry over. `--new` starts a separate deck.

- **Free** keeps the **latest** version (a re-push replaces it; the URL and stats are
  preserved).
- **Pro / Team** keep **full version history** with one-click **rollback**: open a deck's
  **Versions** panel to see every version, preview one (`/d/:id?v=N`), or make an older
  version current.

**Delete a version.** Owners and admins can delete a single version from the menu next to
it in the **Versions** panel, or with `DELETE /api/v1/decks/:id/versions/:n`. Its file, its
thumbnails and its `?v=N` link are gone for good; the deck and its other versions stay. The
current version cannot be deleted: make another one current first (the API answers
`409` with code `current_version`).

**Deleting a deck** removes everything that belongs to it: every version, its view counts,
live sessions and their results, its source editing history and its assistant conversation.

## Live source editing

A deck pushed with `liebstoeckel push --source` keeps its source files in the cloud: team
members edit them together in the dashboard (**Edit sources**), the assistant works on
them, and a developer mirrors them into their own folder with `liebstoeckel dev --live`
(or merges with `pull`). See the [CLI reference](/reference/cli/#pull-and-sync).

**Which files sync.** Text sources only, and never files your Git ignores or files named
like credentials (`.env*`, `*.pem`, `*.key`, `*secret*`, `*credential*`, SSH keys,
`.npmrc`). The CLI prints the ones it left out.

**Secrets are refused.** Every upload is checked for credentials (private keys and tokens
of well-known providers): `push --source` stops with the file and line (code
`secret_found`) before anything is uploaded, and `dev --live` holds such a file back until
you fix it. If a key is pasted in the dashboard editor, it is removed again at once, before
it is saved or reaches anyone else, and the editor says where and why. Keys belong in
environment variables, not in slides.

**History.** Edits turn into checkpoints on their own (after a short pause, and on **Save
version**), credited to whoever edited. Checkpoints older than **90 days** are combined into
one, without the names of who made them; newer ones stay as they are, and folders based on
a recent checkpoint keep merging normally.

**Purge the history.** If a password or key ended up in a deck, take it out of the files
first, then an owner or admin can **Purge history** on the deck page (or
`POST /api/v1/decks/:id/history/purge` with optional `versions` and `assistantChat`). Every
earlier state of the sources and every editor's name are removed; the deck keeps its current
files. You can also delete chosen older versions and the assistant conversation in the same
step. A purge cannot reach copies that already left: developers' own repositories after
`sync commit`, and files people downloaded. Change the leaked credential too.

After a purge, a developer's folder no longer finds the checkpoint it was based on. `pull`
and `dev --live` say so and merge against the live files as they are: identical files stay,
files that differ get conflict markers to resolve, and nothing is lost.

## View analytics

Every deck shows **view counts** (total and unique), for both private opens and public
share links. Counts are privacy-preserving (no raw IP/UA is stored). Expand a deck to see a
**14-day history** strip on paid plans; the free plan shows counts only.

Public share links also carry a **view cap** on the free plan, a built-in guardrail against
abuse that doubles as the upgrade nudge; paid plans are uncapped.

## Plans

Workspaces are **free** by default. Paid plans (**Pro** / **Team**) unlock uncapped share
views, view history with longer retention, public links without the liebstoeckel banner,
longer/no-expiry links, and
larger live sessions with **persisted results**.
Plans attach to the **organization** (one subscription per workspace). The CLI shows your
current plan under `liebstoeckel orgs`. **Pro** is for your personal workspace and **Team**
for a team workspace, from two seats.

### Assistant credits

The hosted assistant spends **credits**: one credit is a tenth of a US cent of model cost,
counted exactly per request. A deck from scratch costs about **3 credits** with today's
model, so the Billing page and the assistant show every amount with "about N decks",
worked out from what your own decks have cost so far.

| Plan | Credits | About |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 15 a calendar month | 5 decks |
| Pro | 100 per billing period | 30 decks |
| Team | 50 per seat per billing period, one pool for the team | 15 decks per seat |

A paid plan's credits renew on the day it renews, and they do not carry over. Owners and
admins see what each member spent this period on the Billing page. When the credits run out,
the assistant stops and says so; your deck stays as it is.

**More credit**, for owners and admins of a paid plan:

- **Monthly credit add-on**: Credits S (500 a month, 5 EUR), M (1,100, 10 EUR) or L (2,400,
  20 EUR), on the same subscription as the plan. A larger size starts at once: you pay the
  difference for the rest of the period and get the difference in credits.
- **One-off credit**: 200 credits for 2 EUR, as many blocks as the workspace's limit
  allows. They are yours at once, until the end of the billing period, and the amount is
  added to your next bill. An owner sets the limit per billing period on the Billing page
  (10 EUR unless changed). One-off credit cannot be added while the plan is set to end.

The assistant's out-of-credit card offers one-off credit first and a larger add-on second.

### Changing seats or the add-on

More seats and a larger add-on start at once and are charged for the rest of the period. The
confirmation shows the amount before anything is charged; amounts too small for the payment
provider to charge go through free.

**Fewer seats, a smaller add-on or none** start with your **next bill**. Until then you keep
what you paid for: the seats (and the invitations they allow) and this period's credits. The
Billing page shows the change as scheduled, with its date, and **Undo** takes it back before
then. Seats never go below the people in the team plus open invitations.

### Cancelling a plan

Owners and admins cancel from the workspace's **Billing** page (**Cancel plan**), or in the
payment provider's portal linked from there. You can also cancel without signing in, on the
**Verträge hier kündigen** page linked from the sign-in screen: enter the email you sign in
with and, optionally, the workspace's name. A confirmation arrives by email either way.

A cancelled plan runs until the end of the month you paid for; nothing more is charged. Until
then, **Keep my plan** on the Billing page undoes the cancellation. When the page without
sign-in is used, every owner of the workspace, and the address the payment provider bills if it
is a different one, also gets a mail with the end date and the way to keep the plan. After that the workspace
moves to the free plan: your decks stay, the free limits apply, and saved live results are
deleted as described above. The confirmation before you cancel says how many past sessions
that affects.

The page without sign-in runs a short check in your browser that you are not a bot while it
opens (a proof of work, with no cookies and nothing sent to anyone else), so sending the
cancellation still takes one click once the check shows as done.

### Withdrawing within 14 days

Subscribed less than 14 days ago? On the same page, **Vertrag widerrufen** withdraws from the
contract instead of cancelling it. Enter the email you sign in with and, optionally, the
workspace's name, then press **Widerruf bestätigen**. The confirmation arrives by email at
once, and every other owner of the workspace gets a mail saying someone withdrew.

A withdrawal is carried out 48 hours after it was received: the plan ends and every payment
for it is refunded in full to the card or account you paid with (the refund can take a few
days to show). Until then, **Keep my plan** on the Billing page stops it, and the plan carries
on as before. A mail confirms when the withdrawal is carried out. If the 14 days have already
passed, the request counts as an ordinary cancellation at the end of the paid month, and the
confirmation says so.

After a withdrawal is carried out, the workspace's saved live results are kept for **30 days**
instead of being deleted with the move to Free, and you can still open them. Choosing Pro or
Team again within those 30 days keeps them for good. The mails and the Billing page give the
date.

### Payments and refunds

If a renewal payment does not go through, the owners and admins of the workspace get a mail
and the Billing page says so. The plan keeps running while the payment provider tries the card
again over the next days; update the card under **Manage payment and invoices** on the Billing
page. If every try fails, the workspace moves to Free.

A workspace has one subscription. If a second one is bought for the same workspace by
accident (two checkouts at the same time), it is ended at once and refunded in full, and the
owners and admins get a mail saying so. When a payment is refunded, or disputed with your bank,
the assistant credits it bought are taken back.
